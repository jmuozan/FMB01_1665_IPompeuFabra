Module 1: Reflection and Key Takeaways (24-10-2026)

Previous CLIL Thoughts and Experience

Before reading the article I had no idea about the CLIL methodology. I did not even know what the acronym stood for, so my conception has clearly evolved.

Some of the insights I gained:
- CLIL is not a method. There is no fixed list of steps or rules; it is an approach based on principles.
- CLIL can be unfair. I had not imagined that it could segregate students
- Teacher training is essential.

A good L2 level is not enough, and teachers need specific training

PBL Methodology and Its Fit with the CLIL Approach

I think it does. A project gives a real purpose to the use of the L2, because students need the language to research, discuss, make decisions and present a final product.
The risk is that the project becomes only a task to be done and the language is not planned, so the linguistic support must be designed on purpose

As key elements of PBL I do consider:
- A meaningful, real-life driving question or challenge
- Clear content and language objectives
- Student voice and choice, so that they feel ownership of the project
- Continuous feedback, reflection, and revision of the work
- A final product that is shared with an audience

Key Takeaways from Module 1

-Scaffolding, comprehensible input, participation and feedback are essential in the CLIL
-The L1 should neither be prohibited nor overused.

Module 2: Assessment in CLIL (05/11/2026)

Assesment tools that I would like to use

I honestly have used all the tools proposed at least once, I would like though to start using the autoevaluation more, especially in the age of AI where personal reflection should be always present.

Highlights of the module

The main ideas I take from the module are the integration of multyassesment methods; the justification of every grade assigned and activity proposed, as Neus mentions in one of the videos, so the student not only gets feedback from what he or she does but also knows exactly what is the goal of each task; and lastly, for group projects, the idea justification that Paul proposed, where the student SHOULD justify each proposal done.

What do you find most challenging when planning a PBL project?

For me there's two important aspects, first, the fact on how to breack down the project in the different tasks to be able to evaluate; and the second, how to individualize assesment between the project participants, which I think is the most essential part and usually it gets overlooked.
